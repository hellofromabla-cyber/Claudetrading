# Timing

Two different questions get lumped together as "timing." Separate them.

## Market timing (trying to buy low, sell high)

Explain gently but directly: consistently predicting short-term market
moves is extremely difficult even for professionals, and a new investor
waiting for "the right moment" often ends up waiting indefinitely, or
buying in emotionally after a rally (fear of missing out) and selling in a
panic during a dip — the opposite of the intended strategy. If the user's
plan depends on correctly timing entries and exits, flag that as a risk to
the plan, not a feature of it.

## Phasing money in: lump sum vs. dollar-cost averaging (DCA)

- **Lump sum** — investing the full available amount at once. Historically,
  markets rise more often than they fall, so on average a lump sum invested
  immediately has outperformed phasing it in gradually — but that average
  hides the downside case: if a downturn follows immediately, the full
  amount takes the full hit at once.
- **Dollar-cost averaging** — splitting the amount into equal chunks
  invested at regular intervals (e.g. monthly) regardless of price. Trades
  away some average return for a smoother ride and less regret risk — useful
  for someone with lower risk tolerance or investing a large lump sum they'd
  find hard to watch drop right after committing it.

Neither is objectively "correct" — which one fits depends on the risk
profile from the previous step, and on the ongoing-contribution budget
already established (an investor contributing from each paycheck is doing a
form of DCA by default, which is worth pointing out).

## Horizon-based timing

As the goal date approaches (see `goals-and-budget.md`), the acceptable
level of volatility usually decreases — this is why target-date and
lifecycle strategies gradually shift allocation from higher-volatility
assets (equities) toward lower-volatility ones (bonds, cash) as the goal
nears. Flag this as something to build into the strategy (see `strategy.md`)
rather than a one-time decision.
