# Strategy

The strategy is the written set of rules that ties the earlier decisions
together and — critically — keeps the plan on track when emotions run high
during a market swing. A plan without written rules tends to quietly become
"whatever feels right in the moment," which is a common way good plans fail.

## Dimensions to define

- **Active vs. passive** — actively picking/trading individual holdings to
  try to beat the market, vs. passively holding broad funds that track it.
  Be honest that passive approaches are lower-effort and, over long periods,
  have historically been hard for most active approaches (professional or
  amateur) to consistently beat after fees — this is context for the
  tradeoff, not a rule that active investing is never appropriate.
- **Buy-and-hold vs. periodic trading** — how often positions actually
  change. Frequent trading based on short-term news or price moves tends to
  increase costs (fees, taxes on realized gains) and is where behavioral
  mistakes (panic-selling, chasing rallies) tend to creep in.
- **Contribution schedule** — tying back to the budget and timing
  decisions: is money added on a fixed schedule (reinforces DCA and
  removes a re-decision each time), or ad hoc?
- **Rebalancing rule** — how and when the portfolio gets brought back to
  its target allocation as holdings drift (e.g. "once a year" or "whenever
  any asset class drifts more than 5 percentage points from target").
  Without this, a portfolio that started at the intended risk level tends
  to drift toward whatever's been growing fastest, quietly becoming riskier
  or more conservative than intended.
- **Sell rule, if any** — under what conditions (if any) a holding gets
  sold outside of rebalancing: a stop-loss level, a fundamental change in
  the goal, reaching the goal itself. Explicitly deciding "I will not sell
  based on short-term price drops alone" is itself a valid and common rule
  for long-horizon, passive plans.
- **Review cadence** — how often the whole plan (not just the portfolio) is
  revisited — e.g. annually, or after a major life event (new job, house
  purchase, market crash) — since goals, budget, and risk tolerance can all
  shift over time.

## Writing it down

Encourage the user to actually write the strategy rules down (the research
brief in `assets/research-worksheet.md` has a slot for this) rather than
keep them as intentions. A written rule is something to hold yourself to
during a downturn; an intention is something anxiety can talk you out of.
