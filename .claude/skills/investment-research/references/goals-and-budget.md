# Goals and Budget

## Setting the goal

Ask enough to pin down three things:
- **Purpose** — retirement, a house deposit, a child's education, general
  wealth building, extra income, capital preservation
- **Target date or horizon** — a specific year, or "flexible," or "never
  really planned to withdraw it"
- **Target amount**, if they have one — not required, but useful for sanity-
  checking whether the budget and expected returns are realistic

A goal 2 years out and a goal 30 years out call for almost opposite asset
choices later, so don't let "just grow my money" stand without at least a
rough horizon attached to it.

If there are multiple goals (retirement *and* a house deposit in 3 years),
treat them as separate buckets with separate horizons and risk profiles —
bundling them into one plan usually means the short-term goal ends up
exposed to volatility it can't afford.

## Setting the budget

Before allocating anything to investing, check for two things that should
come first:

1. **Emergency fund** — commonly discussed as 3–6 months of essential
   expenses in cash or cash-equivalent savings. Investing before this exists
   means a market downturn and a job loss or unplanned bill at the same time
   can force selling investments at the worst possible moment.
2. **High-interest debt** — paying down debt at, say, 20% interest is a
   guaranteed return that most investments won't reliably beat. Ask whether
   there's debt like this before recommending money go into markets instead.

Once those are covered, the budget question is really two questions:
- **Lump sum available now**, if any
- **Ongoing contribution** — a fixed amount per pay period is usually more
  sustainable than "whatever's left over," and enables the timing approach
  covered in `timing.md` (dollar-cost averaging)

A commonly cited starting heuristic (not a rule) is budgeting frameworks
like 50/30/20 (needs/wants/savings-and-investing) — useful as a prompt for
"have you worked out what you can actually spare," not as a number to assert
is correct for this specific person.

Flag clearly: only money the person can afford to have tied up for the full
horizon — and ideally afford to lose some of, since investing carries risk —
belongs in this budget. Rent money, next month's bills, and the emergency
fund itself do not.
