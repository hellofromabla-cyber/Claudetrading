# Diversification Plan

The point of diversifying is that holdings which don't all move together
cushion the portfolio when any one of them drops — the goal isn't to
maximize the number of holdings, it's to reduce how much a single company,
sector, country, or asset class failing can hurt the overall plan.

## Layers to diversify across

Walk through each layer and check the plan isn't concentrated at any of them:
1. **Across companies** — a single stock vs. a fund holding hundreds
2. **Across sectors/industries** — all tech, or spread across sectors that
   don't all rise and fall together
3. **Across asset classes** — all equities, or a mix of equities, bonds,
   cash, and possibly real estate/commodities
4. **Across geographies** — all home-market, or spread across countries/regions
5. **Across time** (see `timing.md`) — investing it all at once vs. phasing
   it in, which diversifies against a single bad entry point

## Turning this into an actual allocation

Once the risk profile, goal horizon, and market/asset choices are known,
diversification becomes a concrete split — e.g. "70% broad global equity
index / 20% bonds / 10% cash," or "60% home-market equity index / 20%
international equity index / 20% bonds." There's no single correct split;
it should visibly trace back to the earlier decisions:
- Longer horizon + higher risk tolerance → generally more equity
- Shorter horizon or lower risk tolerance → generally more bonds/cash
- Any single-stock or single-sector bet → should generally be a small slice
  of the total, not the core of the plan, unless the user has explicitly
  chosen a higher-risk, concentrated approach with eyes open

## Common pitfalls to flag

- **False diversification** — holding 10 different tech stocks feels
  diversified by count but isn't, since they're all exposed to the same
  sector risk.
- **Over-diversification** — so many overlapping funds that the portfolio
  is effectively just an expensive, hard-to-track version of one broad
  index fund. More holdings isn't automatically better once real
  diversification is already achieved.
- **Correlation isn't fixed** — assets that historically moved
  independently can become correlated during a crisis (many things fall
  together in a severe downturn). Diversification reduces risk, it doesn't
  eliminate it.
- **Rebalancing drift** — the split chosen today won't stay put as
  different assets grow at different rates; note that maintaining the
  target split over time is a strategy decision (see `strategy.md`), not a
  one-time setup step.
